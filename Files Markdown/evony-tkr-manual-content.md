## 1. Game Overview

Evony: The King's Return (often shortened to **Evony TKR**) is a mobile/PC city-building and strategy MMO set in a medieval-fantasy world blending real historical figures (as "Generals") with kingdom management, army building, and large-scale PvP warfare. It's the modern remake of the original browser game *Evony*.

Core gameplay loop: build and upgrade a city, gather and manage resources, train an army made of four troop types, recruit and develop historical Generals to lead that army, research technology in the Academy, and use all of that to gather resources on the map, fight monsters (PvE), and battle other players and alliances (PvP) — including massive server-wide events.

The game is designed around long-term progression (accounts run for years) and heavy social/alliance play — a solo player without an alliance is at a severe disadvantage past the early game.

**Suggested page intro blurb:** *"Build your empire, raise legendary Generals, and conquer the battlefield in Evony: The King's Return — a deep city-builder and war-strategy MMO where every decision, from your first Keep upgrade to your Server War rally, shapes your path to becoming a true Monarch."*

## 2. Getting Started — New Player Priorities

What a brand-new Monarch should focus on in roughly the first 1–4 weeks:

- **Finish the tutorial quest line and Main Quests** — they hand out huge amounts of free resources, speedups, and your first Generals. Don't skip steps.
- **Keep upgrading is priority #1.** The Keep (City Hall / Castle) governs the maximum level every other building can reach, so nothing should outrank it for long.
- **Academy is priority #2**, upgraded right after each Keep level-up — it unlocks research, and research compounds over the entire life of the account.
- **Train and keep troops moving.** Don't let your barracks/camps sit idle; keep queues full at all times.
- **Pick a starting General and level them up** as your "main," even a lower-rarity one — a developed cheap General beats an undeveloped good one, and can be swapped out later without losing much.
- **Join an active alliance immediately.** Alliance Help speeds up every construction and research queue, alliance gifts provide resources/speedups, and alliance territory/tiles are safer to gather on. A player with no alliance misses out on a large fraction of total growth speed.
- **Don't open boxed/crated resources immediately.** Opened resources sit in your warehouse where they can be plundered by attackers and are eaten by troop upkeep. Keep them boxed in your inventory (or the alliance warehouse) until you're ready to spend them on a specific upgrade.
- **Use a Shield/Peace Shield (bubble) when offline for long periods**, especially before you have strong walls or a garrison.
- **Don't waste gems on the in-game Store's resource bundles** — they are heavily overpriced compared to the Black Market and to what your alliance/events give for free.
- **Pick a Culture early** (see Section 4) — it's free to change once, so don't overthink the first pick.

> **Beginner Tip callout:** Rss (resources), speedups, and gems are precious — never spend them reactively. Always ask "is this the best use of this resource right now" before tapping confirm.

## 3. Cultures

Choosing a Culture at the start of the game (and being able to change it later) grants a themed set of permanent bonuses. There are seven Cultures: **Europe, China, Japan, Korea, America, Russia, and Arabia.**

- **Europe** — Strong early-to-mid game pick; helps with boss/monster rallies and reduces costs for upgrading expensive buildings later on.
- **China** — Bonuses to resource production and training large batches of troops; a solid generalist/economy pick.
- **Japan** — Best suited to late-game, attack-heavy Keeps and bulk-crafting expensive equipment.
- **Korea** — Strong mid-to-late game growth culture, particularly good for ranged-troop-focused marches.
- **America** — Boosts gold production and research speed for expensive tech — a strong all-around pick, especially valuable because gold gates almost everything (troop training, research, construction).
- **Russia** — Best for late-game, defense-heavy Keeps; good for bulk trap production and fortification.
- **Arabia** — Good for bulk tribute/gem-related activities and for healing large numbers of expensive wounded troops cheaply.

**Changing Culture:** every Monarch gets one free permanent culture change. After that, permanent changes cost gems (historically ~2000 gems), and there are also temporary culture-change items used to briefly switch culture for a specific event or action, then switch back.

> **Pro Tip:** New/F2P players are often steered toward **America** (gold + research) or **China** (resources + troop training) as safe general-purpose starting cultures; switch to a specialized culture (Japan/Russia) once your build (attack vs. defense) is clear.

## 4. City Building & Construction

- **Keep (Castle):** The central building; its level caps the max level of every other building in the city and unlocks new features as it rises. Always the top upgrade priority.
- **Academy:** Where research happens (see Section 6). Upgrade immediately after each Keep upgrade.
- **Rally Spot:** Where alliance rallies are organized; its level determines the max troops you can send to or join in a rally. Keeping Rally Spot level close to Keep level is recommended so you can use your full army strength in rallies.
- **Barracks / Archer Camp / Stables / Workshop:** Train Ground, Ranged, Mounted, and Siege troops respectively. Keep training queues full.
- **Walls / Trap Factory / Archer Tower:** Core defensive buildings (see Section 10).
- **Hospital:** Determines how many wounded troops can be treated/held after a lost battle instead of dying outright (see Section 10).
- **Warehouse:** Protects a portion of resources from being plundered by attackers; upgrading it raises the protected amount.
- **Watchtower:** Provides scouting/early-warning information about incoming marches, and at higher levels lets you inspect the troop composition/buffs of reinforcing allies.
- **Feasting Hall / Inn / Cottage:** Population, happiness, and Monarch/War-related features.
- **Forge:** Where General equipment/gear is crafted and refined (see Section 8).
- **Relic Chamber, Dragon Cliff / Dragon's Pasture:** Unlock later-game systems — Relics and Dragons (see Sections 9 and 11).

**Construction strategy:**

- Prioritize Keep → Academy → resource-production and troop-training buildings → walls/defense, adjusting for your playstyle (attacker vs. turtle/defender).
- Don't auto-consume speedups on low-value upgrades — save them (especially the big ones) for your Keep and Academy, where each level takes progressively longer.
- Subordinate Cities unlock at **Keep Level 11** and become an additional, important source of city-wide buffs (Section 9).

## 5. Resources

Five resources drive the whole economy: **Food, Lumber (Wood), Stone, Ore,** and **Gems**.

- **Food:** Required to train and, critically, to *feed* your troops. Large armies consume food continuously via troop upkeep — running out causes troops to starve/weaken, so food planning matters as much as food gathering.
- **Lumber (Wood):** Used for most building construction/upgrades and for building Siege units.
- **Stone:** Used for upgrading Walls and other defensive structures; gathers more slowly than food/wood but is essential for a strong defense.
- **Ore:** Used for researching military technology, building higher-tier troops, and crafting General equipment — becomes increasingly important at higher Keep levels.
- **Gems:** The premium currency, usable for speedups, VIP time, gear refinement, Culture changes, and more; obtainable free through quests, events, and alliance activity, or purchased.

**Gathering:** the world map has tiles for each resource type (farms, sawmills, quarries, ore mines, and separate gem mines). Send march(es) out to gather; higher-level tiles yield more per hour. Gathering efficiency is boosted by dedicated gathering Generals (e.g., a maxed Queen Jindeok-type General), gathering-boost research, gathering-focused gear, and Monarch Gear crystals (Light Crystal = food, Wood Crystal = lumber, Thunder Crystal = stone, Golden Crystal = ore).

> **Common Mistake callout:** Unboxing all your crated resources at once. Keep resources boxed in inventory until you're about to spend them on a specific upgrade — unboxed resources above your protected Warehouse cap are visible/plunderable and are also silently drained by troop upkeep.

## 6. Research (Academy)

The Academy research tree is split into four branches: **Advancement, Defense, Military,** and **Medical Aid.**

- **Advancement:** Construction speed, research speed (Typography/Super Typography), gathering, and general economic techs. Early priority — faster construction and faster research compound across the entire game.
- **Military:** Troop attack/defense/HP and unlocks for higher troop tiers. Second priority after Advancement basics, since it directly affects your army's combat strength.
- **Defense:** Wall HP/defense, trap capacity, and defensive troop stats — prioritize this if building a defensive/turtle city.
- **Medical Aid:** Hospital capacity and death-to-wounded conversion rate — prioritize if you expect to take losses often (active PvP players) or want to protect your army size.

**General order of operations:** upgrade Academy right after every Keep upgrade → clear early Advancement techs (Construction, Adv. Construction, Super Construction, Typography, Super Typography) → move into Military → then split remaining research between Defense and Medical Aid based on your build.

Research consumes gold and, at higher levels, **Research Stones** (earned via quests/research-factory activity or crafted with gems and gold). Research time scales down with Monarch's Intellect stat/crown level and your Typography research level.

## 7. Troops

Evony TKR has four troop types, each trained in a specific building:

| Type | Trained in | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Ground (Infantry)** | Barracks | Tanky frontline; strong vs. Ranged; countered by Mounted |
| **Mounted (Cavalry)** | Stables | Fast, primary PvE/monster-hunting troop; strong vs. Ground; countered by Ranged |
| **Ranged (Archers)** | Archer Camp | Strong vs. Mounted; countered by Ground/Siege at range |
| **Siege** | Workshop | Longest range of any unit (especially at T13/T14), fragile but hits hard and carries large resource loads; strong vs. Ranged |

**Rock-paper-scissors relationship (near-equal tier/buffs):** Mounted beats Ground → Ground beats Ranged → Ranged beats Mounted, with Siege generally outranging and countering Ranged. In practice: use **Mounted troops for PvE/monster hunting** (fastest, most efficient), and build a mix of all four for PvP, since real battles run in rounds and range determines who hits first.

**Tiers:** Troops range from T1 up to T13/T14 (higher tiers unlock as your Keep and Academy progress). Troops above roughly T9–T10 can be upgraded in place (e.g., T10→T11→T12→T13→T14) rather than retrained from scratch, which is far cheaper once available. Endgame accounts aim to have large, even numbers of all four troop types at the highest tier they can support.

**Rough troop-count guidance by Keep level (community benchmark, adjust to server age/meta):**

- Keep ≤25: ~5,000–10,000 of each tier/type
- Keep 26–30: ~20,000–50,000 of each tier/type
- Keep 30–33: ~50,000–100,000 of each tier/type
- Keep 33–35: 200,000+ of each tier/type

**Troop layering:** Stacking multiple tiers/types of troops in the same march or defense ("layers") forces an attacker to spend rounds fighting through each layer, giving a big defensive advantage — a core intermediate/advanced PvP concept worth its own manual page.

## 8. Generals

Generals are the historical-figure "heroes" who lead your armies, work as building Duty Officers/mayors, and buff your city.

**Rarity:** Generals have multiple rarity tiers; the highest ("Gold"/Historic Legendary) Generals are the ones worth heavily investing in — most Generals of lower rarity that appear in the Tavern are not worth developing seriously.

**Roles a General can fill:**

- **March/Attack General** — leads a specific troop type (Ranged / Mounted / Ground / Siege) in PvP or PvE marches.
- **Wall/Defense General** — only active while defending your own city against a PvP attack (has no effect while attacking or hunting monsters).
- **Monster Hunter** — specialized for PvE boss fights, valued especially for "double item drop rate" skills.
- **Duty Officer** — assigned to a specific building (Stables, Archer Camp, Barracks, Workshop, Hospital, Rally Spot, Academy, Trap Factory, Keep) to passively boost that building's output.
- **Subordinate City Mayor** — placed in a conquered Subordinate City, where their *debuff*-type skills weaken attackers of that city (debuff strength doesn't depend on the General's own stats, so even an undeveloped General is fine here).
- **Resource Gathering General** — boosts gathering speed/capacity for a specific resource.

**Progression systems:**

- **Level/Enhance:** Standard leveling with experience items.
- **Specialties:** Unlock at General level 25 (Gold Generals get 4 specialty slots, Purple/lower rarities fewer); leveled with Runestones and gems, and each raises a specific combat/utility stat.
- **Skill Books:** Each General can equip up to 4 skill books (levels 1–4), drawn from four categories — Military, Development, Subordinate City, and Enemy Debuff — letting you customize a General's role beyond its default kit.
- **Ascending:** From level 5-star, certain Generals can be "Ascended" up to 10 stars using **Blood of Ares** and General Fragments, unlocking major stat jumps.

**Pairing:** Building a strong account usually means pairing one great primary Attack General per troop type with a Wall/Defense General setup for your city, plus dedicated Duty Officers and Subordinate City mayors — rather than trying to make one General do everything.

> **Note for editors:** "Best General" lists shift with every game balance patch and new General release — treat any specific ranked list as a snapshot to be refreshed periodically rather than a permanent manual entry. Focus the published page on the *systems* (roles, specialties, skill books, ascending) rather than a fixed tier list, and link out to a live community tier-list resource if you want current names.

## 9. Equipment (Gear) & the Forge

Generals can be equipped with gear (helmet, weapon, armor, boots, ring, etc.) crafted and improved at the **Forge**.

- **Crafting:** Gear is built at the Forge using materials (including Dragon Scales from certain bosses/monsters) and gold.
- **Refining:** Each piece of gear has **4 refinement slots**. Refining consumes Refining Stones + gold and rolls a random attribute + random value into a slot (e.g., attack %, HP, gathering speed, march speed — the possible pool depends on the gear slot, e.g. helmets can roll HP/gathering, weapons can roll attack/march speed). Refine repeatedly to chase both the *attribute* you want and a high *roll value* within it (aim for roughly 80%+ of the max possible value on a "good" refine).
- **Gear Sets:** Wearing full matching sets typically grants extra set bonuses on top of individual piece stats.

> **Advanced Tip:** Because refining is random, treat it as a long-term resource sink — don't force-stop at the first acceptable roll if you have refining stones to spare; but also don't burn your entire stone stockpile chasing a single perfect slot early game.

## 10. Defense: Walls, Traps, and the Hospital

- **Walls** are the first layer of city defense — their level and HP/defense stats (boosted via Defense-tree research and Wall Generals) determine how much punishment your city can take before troops start dying in large numbers.
- **Trap Factory:** Builds traps that damage/debuff attackers. Community consensus is that trap effectiveness is fairly minor compared to troop/wall strength — build just enough to satisfy daily activity/point requirements rather than over-investing resources here.
- **Hospital:** Raises how many wounded (as opposed to dead) troops your city can hold after a lost defensive battle. Upgrading the Hospital building plus Medical Aid and Defense-tree research (Adv. Medical Power, Adv. Pharmacy, Medicine) increases both hospital capacity and the death-to-wounded conversion rate. Important caveat: in most open-field PvP (outside of certain Battlefield/SvS mechanics), losing an attack march usually results in troops dying outright rather than being wounded — the wounded mechanic mainly protects your defending garrison.
- **Reinforcements:** Sending troops to help defend an ally's city counts as "reinforcing" (and benefits from reinforcement-specific buffs from gear/research/Generals); joining a rally is a different mechanic and does *not* get reinforcement buffs. A Watchtower upgraded far enough lets you inspect the buffs on incoming reinforcement marches.

## 11. Dragons

Unlocked via the **Dragon's Pasture** (around Keep 24) and **Dragon Cliff** (around Keep 30). Dragons are companions attached to a General that grant passive "Dragon Talent" buffs (always on) plus an "Active Talent" ability usable in battle.

- **Dragon Scales** (needed to craft related equipment/upgrades) drop from specific bosses/monsters (e.g., Fafnir, Viking Icebreaker, Viking Berserker, Viking Chief, Medium Cerberus).
- Dragons can be **refined** across four grades (grey → purple → orange → gold), each refine adding meaningful extra buff value — a major long-term power source for veteran accounts.

## 12. Subordinate Cities

Unlocked at **Keep Level 11**. Subordinate Cities are captured/assigned cities that add to your total power, produce gold and materials, and — crucially — grant a **passive city-wide buff** simply for owning them (buffs stack if you own multiple of the same Culture).

- **Rarity tiers:** Common (White) → Uncommon (Green) → Excellent (Blue) → Legendary (Purple) → Epic (Gold); higher rarity = stronger buff percentage.
- **Policy:** Each Subordinate City can be set to a policy focused on troop training (war-oriented), gold production (economy-oriented), or a balance of both.
- **Mayor:** Assigning a General as mayor lets that General's *debuff* skills apply against anyone attacking that Subordinate City — this is one of the best uses for Generals you wouldn't otherwise develop, since debuff strength isn't tied to the General's own stats.

## 13. Monarch Talents

A separate account-wide talent tree, unlocked starting at **Monarch Level 2** (one talent point earned per Monarch level gained after that).

- Talents grant broad passive account buffs — march speed, healing cost reduction, construction speed, resource gathering, troop load, stamina regen, and more.
- The tree opens up in stages as Monarch Level milestones are hit; at each milestone you choose from a small set of talent options.
- Each chosen talent can be leveled up (up to level 4) by spending additional talent points.
- Two separate **Talent Slots/builds** exist, letting you save two different talent configurations and swap between them quickly (e.g., a "growth" build vs. a "war" build).

**Common early/growth-focused progression path:** Free Construction → Builder (queue) Duration → Research Speed → Healing Cost (or Troop Load) → Stamina Regeneration → Resource Gathering (Critical) → Gathering Boost → Troop Upkeep reduction → In-City HP.

## 14. VIP System

A permanent, level-based progression system (VIP 0 up to **VIP 20**) that grants passive account-wide buffs: construction speed, research speed, march size, extra march slots, troop stat bonuses, and more.

- **VIP Level** (permanent, earned via VIP Points) is separate from **VIP Time/Status** (must be actively "topped up" to be active) — buffs from your VIP Level only apply while your VIP status is currently active.
- **VIP Points:** earned mainly from consecutive daily logins (scaling with streak length and current VIP level), plus quests/events/purchases.
- **VIP Time:** consumable item (30 min / 60 min / 1 day / 7 days / 30 days durations) that keeps your VIP status active; can be earned free or bought with gems (bulk 30-day options are the best gem value).
- **Notable milestones:** VIP 5 grants an extra march slot; VIP 8 grants a meaningful construction-speed boost; VIP 10 grants a 10% research-speed boost that compounds across all future research.

> **F2P Tip:** Maintaining active VIP status (even a lower VIP *level*, kept continuously active) is generally worth more day-to-day than letting it lapse — the construction/research speed alone compounds over the life of the account.

## 15. Alliances

Alliances are the backbone of mid-to-late-game play.

**What alliances provide:**

- **Alliance Help** — members can speed up each other's construction/research queues (a huge time save, essentially free).
- **Alliance Gifts** — periodic reward drops including resources, speedups, and gems.
- **Alliance Science/Tech** — a separate, alliance-funded research tree that buffs all members.
- **Alliance Territory & Resource Tiles** — claimed map zones with higher-level, safer resource tiles for members to gather from.
- **Alliance Shop & Alliance Points** — earn points via alliance activities (help, gifts, wars) and spend them in a dedicated shop for useful items.
- **Alliance Warehouse** — a shared, protected place to stash resources.
- **Rallies** — the core way alliances jointly fight big monsters/bosses and enemy cities; rallying pools troops from many members into a single powerful march.

**War of the Ring / territory control features** (fortresses, resource-point capture, alliance territory war) exist on top of this and reward organized, coordinated alliances — an unaligned or inactive-alliance player will struggle to compete here.

## 16. PvP & Battle Mechanics

- **Troop counter system** (near-equal tier/buffs): Mounted > Ground > Ranged > Mounted, with Siege generally outranging/countering Ranged, and Ground able to defend against Ranged. Think rock-paper-scissors, then let range and buffs tip the actual outcome.
- **Battle staging:** combat plays out in behind-the-scenes rounds based on range — battle starts roughly two tile-lengths beyond the longest-range unit involved (e.g., ~352 vs. 350-range Ranged troops, ~502 vs. 500-range Siege), so higher-range troops (Siege, then Ranged) get to act before slower/shorter-range troops close the distance.
- **Core damage concept:** total damage dealt scales with (Attacker's Attack stat × number of attacking troops) minus (Defender's Defense stat × number of defending troops) — buffs (from research, gear, Generals, Dragons) multiply directly into these numbers, so stacking the right buffs matters as much as raw troop count.
- **Five combat-relevant troop attributes:** Attack, Defense, HP, Speed, and Range.
- **Layering:** running multiple troop tiers/types together (in an attack march or, especially, in city defense) forces the enemy to burn combat rounds against each layer in turn — a well-layered defense meaningfully outperforms a single-tier stack of the same total troop count.

## 17. Events

Evony runs a mix of recurring (daily/weekly) events, periodic themed events, and two major server-wide competitions:

**Recurring events (examples):** daily login/check-in rewards, Monarch Competition-style daily tasks, Civilization Celebration Event, Limited Recruit Event, Historic City Event, Cultural Subordinate City Event, Viking Challenge (a key Skill Book source), Hydra Event, and various boss/monster hunting events (with rewards scaling by difficulty).

**Holiday/seasonal events:** themed around real-world holidays (Christmas, Easter, Thanksgiving) and major Eastern holidays (Lunar New Year), typically with limited-time monsters, shops, and cosmetics.

**Server vs. Server (SvS)** — the flagship recurring competitive event, run roughly every other week, pairing two servers against each other for a full week. It has two linked phases:

1. **Server Monarch Competition (MC):** 6 stages, the first 5 (Resource Gathering, Power Increase, City Development, Troop Training, Monster Killing) occurring in random order, followed by a **Kill Event (KE)** finale (similar scoring to a normal MC stage, but compressed to 48 hours instead of 72 due to the extra stage).
2. **Server War (SW):** Begins after MC; scoring is based on troop kills, Temple occupation, and Monster kills. The **Throne War** (fighting to capture and hold the enemy server's Throne city) opens 24 hours after SW starts — holding the Throne for 5 hours transfers a large point swing to your server. Best-of-7 scoring determines the overall winner.

**All-Star War** and cross-brand collaboration events are larger, less frequent special events layered on top of the regular calendar.

## 18. PvE / Monster Hunting

- Mounted troops are the standard PvE workhorse — cheapest and most efficient per-troop for killing monsters/bosses.
- Ranged and Ground troops can hunt monsters too but typically require notably more troops than Mounted to do the same job, so they're not recommended for early-game monster hunting.
- Siege units are very fragile against monsters and should generally only be brought along once you have enough force to guarantee zero losses; they're more valuable for their large resource-carrying capacity while gathering.
- Boss/Monster Hunter Generals are valued primarily for **double item drop rate** skills (a major source of gear-crafting and upgrade materials), alongside raw Mounted attack stats.
- Big monsters/bosses are typically taken down via **alliance rallies**, pooling many members' troops into one attack.

## 19. Spending & Gem Priorities (F2P-Friendly Guidance)

Whether or not the site intends to cover monetization, a short "how to prioritize limited resources" section is valuable for new players:

- **Never** trade gems for resources/gold via the in-game Store — it's drastically overpriced versus the Black Market, alliance gifts, and events.
- **Keeping VIP active** is one of the best steady uses of gems for a free/low-spend account, since the speed buffs compound daily.
- **General development** (specialties, ascending materials, skill books) is typically the best long-term power investment once VIP is stable.
- **Gear refining** is a good ongoing gem/stone sink once you have decent gear to refine.
- Save premium/rare speedups for **Keep and Academy** upgrades specifically — they get exponentially longer, while lower buildings are cheap to brute-force with regular resources.
- Free gem sources worth funneling toward the above: daily quests, alliance activity/gifts, gem mines on the map, Relic Chamber exploration, and event rewards.

## 20. Common Beginner Mistakes (Quick-Reference List)

- Unboxing all resources immediately instead of keeping them safely crated until needed.
- Not joining an alliance (or joining an inactive one) — missing out on Alliance Help, gifts, and safe territory.
- Spending gems on Store resource bundles instead of the Black Market or events.
- Auto-using speedups on cheap early buildings instead of saving them for Keep/Academy.
- Over-investing in Trap Factory instead of walls, troops, and research.
- Building only Mounted troops and neglecting Ranged/Ground/Siege, then struggling once PvP starts.
- Letting VIP status lapse and losing the compounding speed buffs.
- Ignoring Subordinate Cities and their mayor debuffs once unlocked at Keep 11.
- Developing a low-rarity General heavily instead of using it as a placeholder (Duty Officer/Mayor) until a better one arrives.

## 21. Glossary of Common Terms

- **F2P** — Free-to-Play; a player who spends no (or minimal) real money.
- **KE** — Kill Event; the finale stage of the Server Monarch Competition, focused on eliminating enemy troops.
- **MC** — (Server) Monarch Competition; the first phase of an SvS cycle.
- **SW** — Server War; the second phase of an SvS cycle, where two servers directly battle for territory and the Throne.
- **SvS** — Server vs. Server; the combined MC + SW competitive event between two paired servers.
- **Rally** — A joint attack where an alliance member "sets" a march and others join troops into it, combining forces into one strike.
- **Reinforce** — Sending your troops to help defend an ally's city (distinct from joining a rally).
- **Bubble / Shield** — A temporary protective shield that prevents your city from being attacked.
- **Rss** — Shorthand for "resources" (food/lumber/stone/ore).
- **Coining / Heavy Coiner / Low Coiner** — Community slang for spending real money; "heavy coiner" spends a lot, "low coiner" spends little or nothing.
- **Layering** — Stacking multiple troop tiers/types together in a march or defense to force the enemy through several damage "layers."
- **Duty Officer** — A General assigned to a specific building to passively boost its output.
- **Mayor** — A General assigned to lead/defend a Subordinate City.
