## 1. Introduction & Game Overview

Whiteout Survival is a mobile city-builder/strategy game set in a frozen apocalypse: players lead a settlement of survivors, grow a city around a central **Furnace**, build an army of Infantry, Lancers, and Marksmen, and recruit named **Heroes** to lead them — all while cooperating with (and competing against) other players through **Alliances** and server-wide (**State**) events.

The manual is organized around four layers, the same way the game itself is structured:

**City & Chief progression** is the single-player core — growing the Furnace, constructing buildings, managing the four base resources (Meat, Wood, Coal, Iron), and building up the player's own "Chief" profile (VIP level, Chief Gear, Chief Charms). This is covered in Chapters 2–3 and 7–9.

**Heroes & Troops** are the game's combat layer — recruiting and leveling named Heroes, training and tiering up Infantry/Lancer/Marksman troops, and combining both into effective squads for PvE and PvP combat. Covered in Chapters 4–6.

**Alliance life** ties players together — shared Alliance Tech, cooperative PvE events like Bear Hunt and Crazy Joe, and alliance-vs-alliance competitive events. Covered in Chapters 10–12.

**State-level competition** is the endgame layer — State vs State (SvS) war, King of Icefield, and other cross-state events that determine server-wide leadership and rewards. Covered in Chapter 13.

> **Academy Tip** — New players should treat Furnace level, Command Center, and troop camps as a tightly linked trio rather than upgrading any single one far ahead of the others; a high Furnace level with weak troop camps and a low Command Center produces a city that looks strong but can't actually field an effective army. See Chapter 3 for the fuller progression philosophy.

A note on currency of information: Whiteout Survival updates frequently, and several terms commonly used by players in casual conversation (for example, "Frostdragon Nest," "Doomsday event," "Journey of Wonder," "Battle of the Wildlands") **could not be confirmed as current, correctly-named in-game features** during research — see the flags in each relevant chapter. Where this manual uses a system name, it reflects the terminology most consistently confirmed across current (2026) sources; site implementers should do a quick in-game check on any flagged term before publishing it as final copy.

## 2. Furnace Progression, Buildings & Resources

### The Furnace as the game's clock

The **Furnace** is the Whiteout Survival equivalent of a "Town Hall" — it gates population capacity, other buildings' level caps, and troop tiers. The standard track runs **Furnace Levels 1–30**. At Level 30, players enter the **Fire Crystal Age**: the Furnace and several other core military buildings switch to a second progression track measured in **Fire Crystal (FC) tiers — FC1 through FC10 [VERIFY: current max]** — each broken into four sub-levels (FC1-1 through FC1-4, then FC2, and so on). FC10 is the highest tier found in current sources, reachable around **State/Server Day 500**, though an official social post has teased a possible future troop-tier expansion (T11) that may signal where the next content axis is headed rather than a higher Furnace tier specifically.

> **Common Mistake** — Rushing the Furnace level while leaving the Embassy, Command Center, and troop camps behind. Past Furnace 8 the Embassy caps further Furnace growth directly, and a high Furnace level with weak troop camps produces a city that looks strong on paper but can't field an effective army.

### Resource System

Four base resources: **Meat** (food, from Hunter's Hut, sustains population), **Wood** (from Sawmill, the heaviest early-game consumption), **Coal** (from Coal Mine, critical to keep Furnace upgrades moving), and **Iron** (from Iron Mine, required from roughly Furnace 6 onward). Past Furnace 30, the **Crystal Laboratory** refines large quantities of these four resources into **Fire Crystals**, and later **Refined Fire Crystals**, which fund the Fire Crystal Age progression track.

The **Storehouse** protects a set amount of each resource from being plundered by attackers; anything above that protected cap is vulnerable, especially for players who haven't joined an alliance.

### State Age & State Merger

A **State** (server) follows its own age-based unlock calendar independent of any one player's personal progress — heroes, pets, Fire Crystal tiers, the War Academy (around Day 220–230), and FC10 (around Day 500) all unlock on a state-wide timeline. Tracking your State's age helps avoid over-investing in a system about to be superseded, and helps with pre-saving resources ahead of scheduled unlocks.

When a State becomes too inactive or under-populated, Century Games runs a **State Merger**, folding it into another State. Effects include: player cities relocating to a random spot in the new State under a 24-hour shield, alliance territory bonuses expiring, alliance buildings being removed (with resources refunded), Fortresses/Strongholds becoming unoccupied and contestable again, and all leadership roles being invalidated and needing to be re-earned. Participation is mandatory for everyone in a merging State.

> **F2P Note** — There's no dedicated "catch-up" building or mechanic for new or late-joining players; the practical path is gift codes (some gated by Furnace level), event participation, and joining an active alliance for Alliance Tech bonuses, troop reinforcements, and protection.

## 3. Chief Profile, VIP & Progression Philosophy

### Chief Level

The player's own "Chief" profile levels up separately from VIP status, via Chief XP — notably, Chief XP can be purchased directly with Gems (roughly 2 Gems per 1 Chief XP) in addition to being earned through normal play. Chief Level ties most closely into the Chief Gear and Chief Charms systems (Chapter 8), which unlock at Furnace 22 and 25 respectively.

### VIP System

Twelve VIP levels exist, unlocked via cumulative **VIP Points/XP** that never decrease once earned. VIP 1–7 is reachable through free daily logins and normal play; VIP 8–12 requires substantially more investment, with VIP 12 needing several million cumulative VIP XP. Benefits scale with level: extra march queues, faster construction/research/troop training, resource production boosts, troop stat bonuses, and — notably from VIP 8 onward — Mythic Hero Shards and Skill Manuals in the daily VIP rewards, which is why VIP 8 is commonly cited as the single most important VIP milestone.

> **Priority** — VIP status itself (as opposed to VIP *level*) needs to be kept active via 24-hour/7-day/30-day gem purchases for its benefits to apply — a player can be "VIP 8" on paper and get no benefit at all if the subscription-style status has lapsed.

### General Progression Philosophy

The consistent advice across community guides is a **"rush-then-consolidate"** approach: push hard toward key troop-unlock milestones (notably Furnace 26 for Tier 9 troops), then pause the Furnace push to catch up resource buildings, Research Center progress, and troop camp levels before resuming. Rushing the Furnace alone, without matching Command Center and camp levels, produces a city that can't field the army its Furnace level would suggest.

Two buildings act as quiet, easy-to-overlook gates: the **Embassy** (which caps Furnace growth past level 8) and the **Command Center** (which caps how many troops can be sent in a single march or rally, regardless of how strong those troops are individually). Both deserve regular attention even though they're less visually exciting than the Furnace itself.

*(Sources: Whiteout Survival Handbook VIP Guide; Heaven Guardian VIP Levels & Perks, Chief's House & Orders, and Chief Gear guides; whiteoutsurvival.app Chief Level Guide; U7Buy Building Upgrade Priorities guide.)*

## 4. Heroes: Roster, Rarity & Acquisition

### Rarity & Class

Heroes come in four rarity tiers: **Rare → Epic → Legendary → Mythic**. Higher rarity generally means more available skill slots — Mythic heroes get 3 Exploration + 3 Expedition skills, Legendary/Epic get 3 Exploration + 2 Expedition, and Rare gets 2 + 2 (see Chapter 5 for what these skill families mean). Note: some community content still uses informal "SSR/SR/R" shorthand inherited from earlier in the game's life, sometimes treating Legendary and Mythic as one bucket — the site should standardize on the four-tier Rare/Epic/Legendary/Mythic naming.

Every hero also has a **Class** matching the troop-type counter cycle — **Infantry, Lancer, or Marksman** — and a **Sub-class**: the large majority are **Combat** heroes (boosting battle stats), while a smaller set are **Construction/Growth** heroes (boosting city economy — resource gathering, building/healing speed, resource cost reduction) such as Zinman, Jasser, Seo-yoon, Cloris, and Eugene.

### Roster Size & Generations

New hero "generations" (typically 3 heroes each) release roughly every 80 server-days. As of this research pass the roster spans **17 generations and roughly 60+ named heroes**, with Generation 17 (Aiden, Bertha, Eleanor) the newest as of mid-2026.

> **Academy Tip** — Guides consistently warn against replacing a maxed older-generation hero with a freshly-released, unfinished newer one; newer generations aren't always strictly stronger; several are designed as role-specific specialists (Bear Hunt damage, Arena, Exploration) rather than universal upgrades.

### Acquisition

- **Hero Hall recruitment** — the main gacha-style summon system. Basic/Advanced Recruitment uses **Platinum Keys** for mostly Rare–Epic heroes; Epic Recruitment uses **Gold Keys** and can yield Rare, Epic, and rarely Mythic hero shards. A pull that completes a hero refunds shard value rather than being wasted.
- **Hall of Heroes** — a recurring limited-time event specifically for chasing Mythic heroes, using a currency called Marks of Valor.
- **Hero's Mission** — a roughly 3-day event where defeating beasts on the world map has a chance to drop "Scattered Parts" that convert into Mythic hero shards.
- **Journey of Light** — an irregular event sending timed Expeditions to open chests, a good source of Hero Gear materials and Mythic hero shards.
- **Hero Shards** (hero-specific or General/rarity-matched) unlock and star-up a hero; obtained via recruitment, events, chests, Bear Hunt, and SvS event shops.
- **Hero Widgets** — a separate resource used only to craft/upgrade a hero's Exclusive (Special Weapon) Gear, mainly obtained from packs, the Mystery Shop, or the Enigma Event — the most monetization-gated resource in the hero system.

*(Sources: Whiteout Survival Wiki (wiki.wosforge.org) — Heroes page; whiteoutdata.com Exploration vs Expedition and Hero Shards pages; BuffHub Hero Tier List 2026; Aeonizer Hero Recruitment Guide; Heaven Guardian Widgets & Hero Gear Guide; Whiteout Survival Wiki Hero Hall, Journey of Light, Hero's Mission pages.)*

## 5. Hero Skills, Gear & Squad Composition

### Two Skill Families

This is the core hero mechanic worth explaining clearly on the site, since it's a frequent point of player confusion:

- **Exploration Skills** (up to 3, hero-only) — active abilities used in hero-only battles: the Exploration tab, the Arena, and the Lighthouse's "Hero's Journey" mode. No troops are involved; battles are 5 heroes vs. 5 heroes, playable auto or manually.
- **Expedition Skills** (up to 3, mostly passive troop buffs) — active only when heroes are leading troops: world-map marches, rallies (including boss rallies), gathering, and city-vs-city or state events. Construction/Growth-subclass heroes have Expedition skills that buff city economy even when not deployed in combat.

Both skill families are leveled with rarity-matched Skill Manuals (Rare heroes need Rare manuals, and so on, with separate manual pools per skill family).

### Hero Gear (Exclusive/Special Weapon Gear)

Once a hero is fully starred up, it unlocks an **Exclusive ("Special Weapon") Gear** slot, crafted and upgraded with **Hero Widgets** (and, for Mythic heroes pushed past the normal cap, **Essence Stones**). This gear buffs the hero's own stats plus troop-wide "Command" bonuses, and unlocks two additional bonus skills as it's upgraded. This is distinct from **Chief Gear** (Chapter 8), which is worn by the player's own Chief avatar and buffs all troops regardless of which heroes are leading them — the two systems are easy to conflate but are mechanically separate.

### Squad Composition

- **Expedition squads** (marches, rallies): built from one Infantry + one Lancer + one Marksman hero, each leading the matching troop type, to fully leverage the Infantry→Lancer→Marksman→Infantry counter cycle. A commonly cited attacking ratio is roughly 50% Infantry / 20% Lancer / 30% Marksman; a defensive/garrison ratio skews closer to 60% Infantry / 40% Lancer / 0% Marksman, since Marksmen contribute little to garrison kill counts.
- **Rally Captain mechanic:** whoever starts a rally picks the 3-hero squad whose Expedition skills determine the buffs granted to everyone who joins — up to 9 stacked buff types. This makes "who leads the rally" a distinct strategic decision from a normal solo march lineup.
- **Exploration battles** (Exploration tab, Arena, Hero's Journey): 5-hero formations, no troops involved.
- **Garrison defense:** a separate hero role for holding/defending a building; some heroes are specifically tuned for this role rather than offense.

> **F2P Note** — Chief Order (via the Chief's House) and Chief Gear/Chief Charms (Chapter 8) are *not* hero-specific systems, even though they're easy to lump in with hero progression. Chief Order boosts the whole city temporarily; Chief Gear and Charms buff all troops via the player's own Chief avatar, independent of which heroes are leading a given squad.

*(Sources: whiteoutdata.com Exploration vs Expedition page; Whiteout Survival Wiki Hero Gear page; Whiteout Survival Handbook Hero Gear Guide 2026; TopUpLive Hero Synergy System guide; Whiteout Survival Wiki Advanced Guide to Hero Rallies & Buffs; Century Games Combat FAQ.)*

## 6. Troops & Combat Mechanics

### Troop Types & Counter System

Three troop types form a rock-paper-scissors loop: **Infantry beats Lancer, Lancer beats Marksman, Marksman beats Infantry.**

- **Infantry** — front-line tank, high defense/durability, positioned in the front row.
- **Lancer** — balanced mobile/burst unit, positioned in the middle row; some skills let Lancers bypass the front row to strike Marksmen directly.
- **Marksman** — highest raw attack but lowest durability, positioned in the back row.

A commonly cited baseline attacking ratio is 50% Infantry / 20% Lancer / 30% Marksman, adjusted to directly counter whatever the enemy fields. Combat is turn-based: both sides pick targets and attack simultaneously each turn, with units generally targeting the enemy's front row first and only advancing to the next row once it's cleared.

### Troop Tiers & Upgrading

Troops are tiered from T1 up through the current ceiling — as of this research pass, **T1–T10 (standard), then T11 "Helios," then T12 "Exalted"** (the newest tier, live since April 2026). Camps (Infantry/Lancer/Marksman) gate raw tier access as they level up; the **War Academy** governs unlocking T11/T12 specifically via its own tech tree (see Chapter 7 for how this differs from the main Research Center).

Two distinct upgrade concepts are worth keeping separate on the site, since they're a common point of player confusion:

- **Promotion** — once a troop camp reaches its promotion threshold, existing lower-tier troops can be converted directly into the highest currently-trainable tier, rather than only training brand-new troops from T1 up.
- **Fire Crystal upgrade** — once a camp is leveled past 30 using Fire Crystals, it produces a stronger *version of the same tier* (an "FC-tier" troop), not a jump to the next numbered tier. Promotion changes the tier number; the Fire Crystal camp upgrade strengthens the current tier.

T12 "Exalted" specifically requires already owning T11 troops, building a new **Crystal Energy Institute**, and unlocking the War Academy's "Flame Tech" research branch — a genuinely late-game, multi-month (or longer) investment rather than a short-term goal.

### March, Rally & Garrison

- **March/Deployment Capacity** (how many troops one march can carry) scales with Command Center level and Research Center's "Regimental Expansion" line.
- **Formations** — saved troop-ratio and hero presets, unlockable from around Command Center level 10, with several preset slots available for quick reuse.
- **Rally** — multiple players' marches combined against one powerful target (a boss, or an enemy city/fortress). Rally capacity is a separate, larger stat than march capacity. In a rally, the initiator's full hero skill set is active, while each joining player contributes only their first hero slot's skill.
- **Garrison** — troops (plus any alliance reinforcements) defending a city under attack. The defender with the highest stat bonuses is automatically used as the garrison's bonus source, and each reinforcing ally contributes their primary hero skill only. Community guidance treats a defense as "successful" once defenders eliminate over half the attacking force.

> **Academy Tip** — Time reinforcements to arrive *between* incoming attacks, so a garrison stays freshly topped up rather than being caught depleted right before the next hit lands.

### Combat Power

Century Games describes "Squad Power" only as "a special formula based on Hero Power and Troop Level," without publishing exact weights. In practice, Power is a composite of troop tier and count, hero level/stars/gear/skills, Chief Gear, and completed Research — there is no single public formula, so the manual should describe Power as a composite score rather than present an invented equation.

### Healing & Troop Loss

The **Infirmary** holds wounded troops after a costly battle so they can be healed rather than lost permanently; when it's full, a large share of overflow goes to the Enlistment Office and can still be recovered, while the remainder is lost. Casualty rates vary meaningfully by battle type — some PvP battle types have a notable outright-death percentage on the losing side, while several PvE events (Bear Hunt, Frostfire Mine, Foundry Battle) either deal no damage back to players or grant temporarily uncapped Infirmary capacity, removing the risk of permanent troop loss during those specific events.

*(Sources: Century Games Combat FAQ; BlueStacks Troops Guide; TopUpLive Troops Guide; Whiteout Survival Wiki Infirmary and Fire Crystal Infantry Camp pages; Heaven Guardian Infantry Camp Guide; WoSTools War Academy Calculator and T12 Exalted Troops blog; Carry1st T12/SvS Guide 2026.)*

## 7. Research Tree

### Research Center vs. War Academy — two separate tech systems

Worth stating clearly on the site, since the names are easy to conflate: the **Research Center** (unlocked around Furnace 9) is the everyday tech tree covering city growth, economy, and troop combat stats. The **War Academy** (a much later-game building, unlocked around server-day 220+) is a separate, harder tech track specifically for unlocking T11 "Helios" and T12 "Exalted" troops (see Chapter 6). Both are "tech trees," but they gate very different things.

### Research Center: Three Branches

1. **Growth** — internal city mechanics: research speed, construction speed, troop training capacity/queue, march size.
2. **Economy** — resource production for each resource type, including **Gathering Speed**, widely cited as a top-priority node since most late-game resources come from marches gathering on the map.
3. **Battle** — combat power, split into Infantry/Marksman/Lancer sub-branches (attack/defense/HP), plus deployment-capacity nodes like Regimental Expansion.

### Recommended Priority

Broad community consensus favors **Growth → Economy → Battle**, on the logic that faster research and construction speed compounds — every subsequent project in any tree becomes cheaper in time once Growth is ahead. Within Battle, prioritize Infantry first (the frontline tank whose survival lets Marksman damage and Lancer versatility actually matter), then Marksman attack, then Lancer.

> **Academy Tip** — Within an alliance, it's common for members to informally split research focus — some pushing Battle for war-readiness, others covering Economy/Growth — rather than everyone maxing the identical tree at the same pace.

*(Sources: BlueStacks Research Center Guide; AppGamer Research Center and Researching Tech guide; VeloxGame Research Priority Path; LDShop Research Center Guide; Whiteout Survival Handbook Research Center Guide 2026; Heaven Guardian Mastering the Research Center.)*

## 8. Chief Gear & Chief Charms

### Chief Gear (unlocks Furnace 22)

Six gear slots, commonly referred to as **Coat, Pants, Cap, Watch, Belt/Ring, and Weapon [VERIFY exact current slot names in-client — sources vary slightly]**, paired two-by-two to buff Infantry, Lancer, and Marksman respectively (Attack and Defense for that troop type). Equipping 3 pieces of the same quality grants a Defense bonus across all three troop types; equipping all 6 of the same quality grants an Attack bonus across all three.

**Quality tiers, in order:** Green (Uncommon) → Blue (Rare) → Purple (Epic) → Gold (Mythic) → Red (Legendary), each with star sub-tiers and, at the top end, further T-ratings (T1–T4) as investment deepens. Total stat bonuses scale from roughly +9% at the lowest tier past +180% at max Red tier; at Red tier, gear also grants a flat Troop Deployment Capacity bonus on top of the percentage stat bonus.

**Crafting materials:** Hardened Alloy and Polishing Solution are the base materials for all upgrades (from Polar Terror rallies, hunting high-level Beasts, the Crazy Joe event, or Alliance Championship Shop trades); Design Plan is required from Blue tier onward; Lunar Amber is required from Red tier onward. Once one gear piece reaches a high enough Mythic-tier milestone, an Enhancement Material Exchange unlocks, letting players trade these materials for each other.

**Mastery Forging** is a further enhancement path exclusive to Gold (Mythic)-quality gear, requiring Furnace 20 and that piece already at a high enhancement level, using **Essence Stones** for an extra percentage buff. This appears to be the closest current analog to a "gear refinement" system — worth using this term rather than an unconfirmed "Refinement" label.

> **Academy Tip** — Chief Gear (worn by the player's own Chief avatar, buffing all troops) is a different system from Hero Gear (Chapter 5, tied to individual heroes). Keep the two clearly separated in any site copy, since community sources frequently blur them together.

### Chief Charms (unlocks Furnace 25)

Each of the 6 Chief Gear pieces has **3 independent charm slots**, for 18 total, grouped by troop type (Infantry, Marksman, Lancer). Charms have **16 levels total**: levels 1–3 are single-step, levels 4–10 have 4 sub-levels each, and from level 11 onward each level has 5 sub-levels.

**Materials, in order of when they're needed:** Charm Design (earliest, smaller quantities), Charm Guide (needed in larger quantities — prioritize farming these), and Charm Secrets (needed from level 12 through the level-16 cap). All three primarily drop from Frostfire Mine and Castle Battle-type events, and are also purchasable in several event shops. A Charm Material Exchange unlocks once a player has at least one Level 11 charm, letting the three materials be traded for each other.

> **Common Mistake** — Confusing "Charm Design" (a Charm material) with "Design Plan" (the separate Chief Gear material) — these are two different items with similar names.

The Level 16 charm cap is gated by State/server age (roughly "Generation 7" of hero releases), not just personal Furnace level — worth flagging to players that this particular cap is a state-wide gate.

*(Sources: Whiteout Survival Wiki — Chief Gear, Chief Charms, Hero Gear pages; BlueStacks Chief Gear Guide; Heaven Guardian Chief Gear Upgrade Priority and Chief Charm Secrets guides; woscalc.com Chief Gear Guide; WoSTools Chief Charms Guide.)*

## 9. Pet System

Unlocked at **Furnace Level 18** via the **Beast Cage** building. **[VERIFY]** — no source confirmed alternate names like "Battle of the Wildlands" or "Frostdragon" for this system; the in-game feature is simply called "Pets," and the site should use that name rather than either of those.

Pets are tamed sequentially — each new pet requires the previous one to reach a set level before it becomes tameable, effectively gating the full roster behind both Furnace level and cumulative server age (some of the newest pets require 350–450+ days of server age to unlock).

### Roles

Pets split into two functional groups:

- **Development/Support pets** — construction speed, gathering efficiency, stamina restoration, and bonus item drops (e.g., Cave Hyena for build time, Musk Ox for instant resource gathering, Arctic Wolf for stamina restore).
- **Combat pets** — buffs applied to troops during rallies, Bear Hunt, and State-level fights (e.g., Iron Rhino's large temporary rally-capacity boost, near-mandatory for rally leaders; Cave Lion's general troop-attack buff, commonly called the "gold standard" all-purpose combat pet).

### Progression

Pets level up using **Pet Food**, immediately raising troop Attack, Defense, and HP as a base-wide passive effect. Every 10 levels, a pet hits a growth ceiling requiring **Advancement Marks** to break through; the first Advancement (at level 10) is the most important milestone, since it unlocks the pet's Active Skill — before that, a pet contributes passive stat growth only.

> **Academy Tip** — Early game, prioritize Development pets to accelerate general base growth; Combat pets (especially a rally-capacity pet and a general attack-buff pet) become the priority once a player starts leading rallies or focusing on state-level combat.

*(Sources: Whiteout Survival Wiki — Pets page; u7buy Pet Guide 2026; TheriaGames Pet Guide; BlueStacks Pet Tier List 2026; Whiteout Survival Handbook Best Pets Tier List 2026.)*

## 10. Alliance Structure & Alliance Tech

### Roles

Six ranks: **R5 (Leader)** — one per alliance, full control including Alliance HQ management, leadership transfer, and the Alliance Whitelist (a protected-member list); **R4 (Co-Leader)** — nearly all Leader powers except a few top-level settings, can promote/demote R3 and below, start Alliance Tech research, and deploy Alliance Crystal Skills during events; **R3** — can promote/demote R1–R2; **R2** — same base perks as R1 plus alliance-wide broadcast messaging; **R1** — basic accepted-member rights (event participation, Alliance Help, tech donations); **R0** — brand-new/unranked members with minimal access.

### Creating & Joining

The Alliance panel unlocks at Furnace Level 7. Creating an alliance costs Gems below Furnace 10 and is free at Furnace 10+. Joining is done via the Alliance Power leaderboard or a "Recommended Alliance" matching feature based on power, activity, and the target alliance's own recruitment settings.

### Alliance Tech

A shared research tree with three sub-trees mirroring the personal Research Center: **Growth** (member cap, rally sizes), **Territory** (banner count, storage capacity), and **Battle** (rally troop caps, combat bonuses). Members donate resources to a selected tech, earning Alliance Tokens and Alliance EXP in return; tokens are spent in the Alliance Shop for VIP EXP, hero shards, speedups, and similar items.

### Activity & Territory

Alliances track a **Daily Activity Triumph** (reaching a personal points threshold unlocks a shared chest) and a **Weekly Triumph** for alliances with enough active members. **Alliance Territory** is built outward from an Alliance HQ via placed banners; claiming a wild resource farm requires the territory to cover most of that farm's footprint. **Secured Alliance Gathering Nodes** are temporary protected gathering spots placeable within owned territory. Each State map also contains a fixed number of capturable **Fortresses and Strongholds**, contestable only by alliances whose territory borders them (see Chapter 12 for the weekly Fortress Battles event that determines ownership).

*(Sources: medievalfun.com Alliances Guide; onechilledgamer.com Alliance Guide; Out of Games Alliance Whitelist and Secured Gathering Node guides; Whiteout Survival Wiki — Alliance Tech, Alliance Territory pages; Heaven Guardian Alliance Contribution guide; ldcloud.net Alliance Guide.)*

## 11. Alliance PvE Events

### Bear Hunt (Bear Trap)

A pure PvE alliance rally event: a boss (referred to in current sources as the **Raging Bear** — **[VERIFY]**, an alternate name like "Nia the Bear" was not confirmed anywhere) spawns near the Alliance HQ roughly every 47 hours. It deals no damage back to players, making this a risk-free damage race. A rally captain starts the attack and all three of their hero Expedition Skills count toward the rally; joiners contribute only their first hero slot's skill, so flat damage-boosting Expedition Skills in that slot are especially valuable for anyone joining rather than leading. Alliances can donate a specific material to upgrade the Bear Trap building itself, raising the damage bonus available to all participants. Rewards center on **Essence Stones** (used for Chief/Hero Gear upgrades), making this one of the best reliable sources of that otherwise-scarce material.

> **Academy Tip** — Since Bear Hunt can't kill troops, favor ranged/Marksman-heavy compositions for maximum sustained damage rather than a defensively-balanced army.

### Crazy Joe

A wave-defense event: bandits attack players' individual cities and the shared Alliance HQ across roughly 20 waves over about 40 minutes. Waves 10 and 20 target the Alliance HQ directly and are the highest-priority, requiring full alliance coordination. Scoring is based on the percentage of each attacking wave eliminated, and points can also be earned by reinforcing/defending allies' cities. Rewards include gear-enhancement materials, hero shards, and resources, and it is a notable source of Chief Gear crafting materials.

### Frostdragon Tyrant

**[VERIFY]** — current sources consistently use the name "Frostdragon Tyrant" rather than "Frostdragon Nest"; treat these as likely the same event line under its current name, but confirm in-game before publishing. A monthly, large-scale alliance-based event where multiple alliances fight for control of a central Frostdragon Capital, surrounded by four Spires that grant strong temporary buffs when captured. Victory requires cumulatively holding the Capital for a set total number of hours during the contest window. A special currency (earned from event-specific escorts and mining nodes) is spent by alliance leadership (R4/R5) to trigger powerful alliance-wide combat buffs at key moments. Infirmary capacity is uncapped during the event, meaning participation carries little risk of permanent troop loss.

*(Sources: joytify.com and onechilledgamer.com Bear Hunt guides; whiteout-survival.com Bear Hunt Event Guide; outof.games Bear Hunt overview; BlueStacks Crazy Joe Event Guide; csrlm.com Crazy Joe Guide; theriagames.com and heaven-guardian.com Frostdragon Tyrant guides; Whiteout Survival Wiki — Bear Hunt, Frostdragon Tyrant event pages.)*

## 12. Alliance Competitive Events

### Alliance Championship

A weekly alliance-vs-alliance tournament: six alliances compete across five rounds. The battlefield has three lanes, each with a capturable flag and a minimum combatant requirement; winning two of three lanes wins the matchup. Alliances carry a persistent tier that rises with consistent good performance, unlocking better reward pools over time.

### Alliance Mobilization

A biweekly cooperative event combining individual daily task completion with alliance-wide point totals. Players choose from offered tasks each day (color-coded by point value, with rare "Exclusive Missions" worth significantly more), building toward both a personal reward tier and a shared alliance tier. Troop Training and stamina-based Monster/Terror Hunting are commonly recommended as the most efficient, F2P-friendly task categories.

### Fortress Battles

A weekly event (Fridays) where alliances fight for control of a State's 16 total capturable structures (12 Fortresses + 4 Strongholds). Alliance leadership applies for specific structures during a registration phase; ownership is decided in a scheduled battle window, requiring continuous control for a set duration to formally claim a structure.

### Foundry Battle

A biweekly, larger-scale competitive event (alternating on the calendar with the solo Frostfire Mine event), pitting alliance "legions" of around 40 members against each other to capture and hold buildings for points, over a roughly hour-long window.

### Alliance Showdown

A monthly, multi-day cross-state event for an alliance's top-ranked competitors (roughly the top 20 alliances by Alliance Power), matched 1-on-1 against a rival alliance across several themed daily stages (escort/raid missions, Fire Crystal spending, hero upgrades, troop training, pet upgrades, gear material spending), culminating in an extended final "Full-Scale Competition" stage where all prior activities count simultaneously.

### Wild Brawl

An irregular, individual (rather than team) PvP event: ten Chiefs compete free-for-all on a shared battlefield, earning points by occupying buildings and gathering strategic supplies, spendable in a dedicated event shop.

> **Advanced** — All of the events in this chapter reward consistent participation over occasional high-effort pushes; alliances that coordinate task/lane/stage assignments in advance (via alliance chat or an external tool) consistently outperform those that improvise on the day.

*(Sources: ldshop.gg and BlueStacks Alliance Championship guides; BlueStacks and Whiteout Survival Wiki Alliance Mobilization pages; Whiteout Survival Wiki and onechilledgamer.com Fortress Battles guides; u7buy and BlueStacks Foundry Battle guides; ldshop.gg and wostools.net Alliance Showdown guides; Whiteout Survival Wiki Wild Brawl page.)*

## 13. State vs State (SvS) & King of Icefield

### State vs State ("State of Power")

The biggest recurring PvP event in the game — a full cross-state war between two matched states, run roughly every 4 weeks (alternating in the calendar with King of Icefield), requiring Furnace 16+ and a State that's at least roughly 12 weeks old.

**Four phases:** a **Matchmaking Phase** (~2 days) pairs two similarly-matched states; a multi-day **Preparation Phase** runs a series of themed daily stages (construction, hero/research development, beast-slaying, troop training) each with its own leaderboards and milestone rewards; a 12-hour **Battle Phase** opens cross-state PvP, centered on the **Sunfire Castle Battle** — holding the castle continuously for a set duration is an automatic win, otherwise the side with the longest cumulative hold wins; and a final **Field Triage Phase** allows recovery.

Winning the Preparation Phase grants a healing/capacity buff for the Battle Phase and shifts the Sunfire Castle Battle to be fought on the losing state's own territory (home-field advantage flips to the Prep-phase winner).

**Ministry system:** rather than an automatic state-wide reward, winning SvS grants the winning alliance's leadership appointment rights over a temporary "ministry" of roles (President, Supreme President if both phases are swept, and several Ministers), each granting sizeable personal stat or economy buffs to the appointee, plus a small number of state-wide "Authority Skills" the ministry can trigger for everyone.

> **Priority** — Community guidance consistently treats SvS (and Alliance Showdown) as the events to stockpile Speedups, Fire Crystals, and Mithril for, rather than spending them on day-to-day upgrades.

### King of Icefield (KOI)

A 7-day cross-state event alternating with SvS in the same roughly-4-week calendar slot. Six states compete via daily themed tasks (construction, hero development, combat training, and similar), earning a currency redeemable for tiered chests, with both personal and cross-state rankings tracked.

### Frostfire Mine (solo PvP/gathering)

A biweekly, 30-minute solo event (alternating with the alliance-scale Foundry Battle) where players mine a rare crafting resource from a three-ring map, with other players able to attack miners at any time — defeating enemy patrol guards yields large rewards, making it as much a PvP-skirmish event as a gathering one.

*(Sources: Whiteout Survival Handbook State vs State Guide 2026; wostools.net SvS Reference Wiki; BlueStacks SvS Guide and Recurring Events Guide; BlueStacks King of Icefield Guide; Whiteout Survival Handbook King of Icefield Rewards Guide 2026; BlueStacks Frostfire Mine Guide; Whiteout Survival Wiki Tips for Frostfire.)*

## 14. Economy: VIP, Gems & Events Calendar

### Gem Economy

Gems are used for VIP XP purchase and VIP status activation, hero recruitment pulls, permanent account unlocks (like extra march queues), event-milestone spending, and general shop purchases. Free gem sources include daily/growth missions, alliance participation (Alliance Tech contribution and ranking rewards), and general event/expedition rewards.

**Recommended spending priority**, per community consensus: VIP progression up to VIP 8 first (the level where Mythic Hero Shards start appearing in daily rewards, and where key queue/slot unlocks tend to cluster); hero recruitment pulls during banner events next; then permanent account-wide features (extra March Queue and similar one-time unlocks). Impulse speedup purchases and non-essential refreshes outside major events are generally not recommended.

> **F2P Note** — A Monthly Card-style purchase (a small one-time or recurring purchase granting a lump sum of gems plus a daily drip for 30 days) is consistently cited as far better gem-per-dollar value than buying gems directly from the standard shop, and is a commonly recommended first small purchase for otherwise F2P players.

## 15. Glossary & Quick Reference

### Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Furnace | The central city building, equivalent to a "Town Hall" — gates population, other buildings, and troop tiers. |
| FC | Fire Crystal — both the post-Furnace-30 tier naming (FC1–FC10) and the resource used to fund it. |
| RFC | Refined Fire Crystal — a further-refined resource needed for later FC tiers and T12 troops. |
| State | The server a player belongs to; follows its own age-based content unlock calendar. |
| Chief | The player's own avatar/profile, distinct from any recruited Hero — levels via Chief XP and equips Chief Gear/Charms. |
| Hero | A named character providing passive city/troop bonuses and active combat skills; distinct rarity/class/sub-class. |
| Exploration Skill | A hero-only active skill used in hero-vs-hero battles (Exploration tab, Arena, Hero's Journey). |
| Expedition Skill | A hero skill active only when leading troops (marches, rallies, gathering, state events). |
| Hero Gear | Equipment tied to an individual hero, crafted with Widgets — distinct from Chief Gear. |
| Chief Gear / Chief Charms | Equipment worn by the player's own Chief avatar, buffing all troops regardless of hero. |
| T1–T12 | Troop tiers, from basic (T1) to the current top tiers T11 "Helios" and T12 "Exalted." |
| Promotion | Converting existing lower-tier troops into the current highest-trainable tier. |
| Rally | Multiple players' marches combined against one powerful target (boss, city, or fortress). |
| Garrison | Troops (plus ally reinforcements) defending a city under attack. |
| R5 / R4 / R3 / R2 / R1 / R0 | Alliance ranks, from Leader (R5) down to unranked new member (R0). |
| Alliance Tech | The alliance's shared research tree, funded by member resource donations. |
| SvS | State vs State — the major cross-state war event. |
| VIP | A permanent level (from cumulative VIP XP) plus a separate, renewable "active status" needed to receive that level's benefits. |
