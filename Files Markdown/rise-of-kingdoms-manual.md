*Content pack for the new game section on Sentinel Game Academy. English-language content, structured to match the site's field-manual format. Info boxes use the same categories as the Age of Z Origins guide: **TIP**, **MISTAKE TO AVOID**, **PRIORITY**, **F2P**, **ADVANCED**.*

*Editorial note: a few terms from the original research brief don't exist in Rise of Kingdoms (they belong to similar-looking games such as Kingshot, Evony, or Lords Mobile) and have been corrected below — see the callouts marked "Terminology note."*

---

## Section 1 — Game Overview

Rise of Kingdoms (RoK) is a mobile 4X strategy game by Lilith Games: eXplore, eXpand, eXploit, eXterminate, built around city development, a Commander/hero system, and large-scale multiplayer warfare.

The feature that most separates RoK from competitors (Lords Mobile, Evony, Kingshot, etc.) is that the entire game — your city, the open world map, and every battle — runs on **one continuous, seamlessly zoomable map**. There is no separate "attack screen": troops move, gather, and fight in real time on the same map you build your city on.

Other defining systems: 15 historical **Civilizations** (cosmetic/stat packages, not unique tech trees), a deep **Commander** system with dual-commander pairing and 74-point talent trees, seasonal cross-server **Kingdom vs. Kingdom (KvK)** warfare, and a **Kingdom Titles** political layer (King, Queen, Duke, Justice, etc.) run by the top players of each kingdom.

> **TIP:** RoK's learning curve is steep because almost every system (civs, commanders, talents, titles, KvK) interacts with every other system. Read this manual section by section rather than trying to optimize everything on day one.

## Section 2 — Getting Started (New Governor Checklist)

Recommended order of priorities for a brand-new city:

1. Pick a starting **Civilization** (Section 3) — this is reversible later (for gems or alliance credits) but costs real resources to change, so treat the first pick as semi-permanent.
2. Rush **City Hall** (RoK's core "town hall" building — see Terminology note in Section 4) while keeping the **Wall** close behind it.
3. Join an **Alliance** on day one — any active alliance beats none. It's normal and expected to switch alliances later once you find the right fit for language, activity level, and playstyle.
4. Start clearing the free Economic tech unlocked by visiting **Villages** on the map — this is a free research shortcut with no downside.
5. Push toward **VIP 6**, which permanently unlocks the second building queue (see Section 6).
6. Farm regular Barbarians and, once you can rally, Barbarian Forts, for Commander XP and Books of Covenant (Section 11).

> **MISTAKE TO AVOID:** Delaying your alliance join to "shop around" for the perfect one. Early alliance help speeds up nearly everything (buildings, research, healing) — joining late just slows your whole account down. Switch later if needed; there's no penalty for leaving.

## Section 3 — Civilizations

Civilizations are **not** unique factions with their own buildings. Each civilization instead grants exactly three things: a free starting Commander, a special Tier 4 troop unique to that civilization (unlocked once you research the relevant Tier 4 tech), and three passive stat bonuses. Buildings also get a civilization-specific visual skin (cosmetic only).

Currently 15 civilizations exist: Rome, Greece, Germany, Britain, France, China, Vikings, Egypt, Japan, Korea, Spain, Arabia, Ottoman, Byzantium, Maya. You can switch civilization later via a Civilization Change item (10,000 gems, or 2,000,000 Alliance Credits from the Alliance Store) without losing buildings, research, or commanders.

### Civilization strength notes

- **China** is widely rated the strongest general-purpose pick, especially for new accounts: the flat +5% building speed compounds across the entire game.
- **Arabia** is the top pick for dedicated rally leaders and KvK play — its +50% rallied-army-damage bonus is one of the single highest-value PvP bonuses in the game.
- **Korea** suits research-heavy, high-investment accounts: research speed plus a large hospital buffer for war losses.
- **Germany** suits growth/economy accounts: faster troop training and action point recovery translate into faster barbarian farming.
- **Japan** and **Spain** are strong farming/gathering civilizations.
- **Byzantium** and **France** are favored by war-heavy accounts thanks to hospital capacity and healing speed.

> **PRIORITY:** If you're unsure, pick China as a new player. It has no glaring weakness and its building-speed bonus pays off from day one regardless of what role you settle into later.

## Section 4 — City Building Priorities

*Terminology note: this manual uses **City Hall** as the core building name — it is the building that gates the level of every other structure in your city, unlocks new building types, troop tiers, and research tiers, and increases march/rally capacity. (There is no building literally called "Command Center" in Rise of Kingdoms.)*

### Recommended build order for new accounts

1. **City Hall** — always your top priority; push it as far as your resources allow.
2. **Wall** — must be kept close to City Hall's level; it's a prerequisite for further City Hall upgrades, it garrisons commanders, and prevents your city from being forcibly relocated if its durability hits zero in combat.
3. **Farm / Lumber Mill / Quarry / Gold Mine** — your resource-production buildings; every other upgrade's cost scales off these.
4. **Storehouse** — protects a flat amount of each resource from being plundered by attackers (see Section 5).
5. **Academy** — you cannot research while it's upgrading, but it's treated as a top-tier permanent priority alongside City Hall since research scales your entire account.
6. **Hospital** — occasionally a direct City Hall upgrade requirement, and increasingly critical once you're active in PvP/KvK, since it protects wounded troops from being permanently lost.
7. **Barracks / Archery Range / Stable / Siege Workshop** — upgrade in parallel with City Hall to unlock higher troop tiers. A common rule of thumb: only push these further once your Hospital capacity can absorb the losses a bigger army implies.
8. **Alliance Center** — governs your Alliance Help capacity, garrison size, and rally/reinforcement capacity.
9. **Scout Camp** and **Trading Post** — smaller secondary buildings worth a quick upgrade pass: faster/farther scouting, and lower tax plus faster resource transfers to allies or farm accounts.

### City Hall milestones

- **CH 4** unlocks the Stable.
- **CH 5** unlocks the Siege Workshop, a 2nd March Queue, the Shop, and a 2nd Lumber Mill.
- **CH 6** unlocks an Academy tier jump, a 2nd Hospital, and the 1st Quarry.
- Mid-game milestones (roughly CH 16, 21, 25) unlock mid- and top-tier troops and extra dispatch/march queues; **CH 25** is the point at which most accounts pivot fully onto Castle and Academy upgrades to chase Tier 5 troops.
- **VIP 6** permanently unlocks the **second building queue** — this is one of the single most commonly recommended "rush this first" milestones for new players (see Section 6).

> **TIP:** Resource-production buildings (Farm, Lumber Mill, Quarry, Gold Mine) only store about 10 hours' worth of output before they cap out. Collect roughly twice a day so nothing goes to waste — production sitting uncollected inside the building is also safe from raids.

### Alliance Center and Alliance Help

Alliance members can send "Alliance Help" to speed up your building, research, and troop-healing queues (not troop training or gathering). Each individual help removes 1% of the remaining time, or 1 minute — whichever is greater. The base cap is 5 helps per queued task, plus 1 extra help per Alliance Center level.

> **MISTAKE TO AVOID:** Leaving a builder, research, or training queue empty "just for a bit." Time, not raw materials, is the scarcest resource in Rise of Kingdoms — an idle queue is wasted progress you can never get back.

## Section 5 — Resource Management

RoK has four core city resources — **Food, Wood, Stone, Gold** — plus the premium currency, **Gems**.

- **Food** feeds troops (upkeep) and is required for training.
- **Wood** covers construction and upgrades.
- **Stone** covers construction and becomes the main bottleneck at higher city levels.
- **Gold** covers research and higher-tier troop training.
- **Gems** buy speedups, VIP points, resource packs, civilization/name changes, and commander sculptures.

### Two ways to gain resources

1. **City production** — your Farm, Lumber Mill, Quarry, and Gold Mine buildings generate resources passively, capped at roughly 10 hours' worth of accumulation; collect about twice a day.
2. **Map gathering** — send troops, led by a Commander, to resource tiles on the world map. Regular kingdom-map nodes commonly top out around level 6 near the map center; endgame Lost Kingdom maps go up to around level 9. Gathering troops are vulnerable to attack while en route or on the tile, so gather close to your city or your alliance's territory and coordinate with alliance members for backup.

### Protecting your resources

- The **Storehouse** sets a flat, non-percentage capacity of each resource type that cannot be plundered — anything stockpiled above that capacity is vulnerable to raiding. Upgrading Storehouse permanently raises this protected capacity.
- Resources still sitting uncollected inside production buildings are also safe from raids.
- Converting excess raw resources into **resource item packs** (via the Courier Station's traveling merchant, events, or the Trading Post) removes them from your raidable stockpile entirely — items in your inventory carry no plunder risk and no storage cap.
- Temporary Shields (from items, events, or new-player protection) blanket-protect your whole city, including uncollected production, for a set duration — separate from Storehouse's permanent cap.

> **MISTAKE TO AVOID:** Converting resource tokens/packs into raw resources before you actually need them. The moment they land in your stockpile they become raidable above your Storehouse cap — keep them "banked" as items until the moment of use.

### Boosting resource output

- **VIP levels** grant stacking passive bonuses to production and gathering speed (Section 6).
- **Civilization bonuses** (Section 3).
- **Academy technology** — direct percentage boosts to gathering and production per resource type (Section 12).
- **Kingdom Titles** — e.g. Prime Minister gives +15% resource production and +10% building speed; Queen gives +15% gathering speed (Section 7).
- **Farm accounts** — many mid/late-game players build up secondary "farm" accounts purely for economy and funnel their production to a main account via the Trading Post (subject to a tax reduced by Trading Post level).

## Section 6 — VIP System

VIP is a permanent stat-buff and daily-rewards system, not a pure pay-wall — it has a genuine free-to-play path, though spending accelerates it substantially.

### Earning VIP Points

- The daily login chest starts at 40 VIP Points and increases by 20/day up to a cap of 200/day, as long as you claim it every day — missing a day resets the streak back to 40.
- 1 gem = 1 VIP Point when buying directly.
- The Alliance Shop sells VIP-point items for Alliance Credits — a genuine F2P route.
- The VIP Shop unlocks once you build the Shop at City Hall level 5.

> **F2P:** Reaching VIP 12 without spending is achievable with consistent daily play; pushing past VIP 12 F2P is slow. Save gems rather than spending them piecemeal, and dump them during the recurring "More Than Gems"-style event (roughly every two weeks), which gives noticeably better VIP-point value per gem than a normal purchase.

### Key VIP milestones

| VIP level | Notable unlock |
|---|---|
| VIP 5 | First Hospital Capacity bonus; +1 free Sunset Canyon attempt |
| **VIP 6** | **Permanently unlocks the 2nd building queue** — the single most commonly cited early "rush" target |
| VIP 10 | ~1.29x resource production; 1 free legendary commander sculpture daily |
| VIP 11 | First Troop Attack bonus |
| VIP 12 | First Troop Defense bonus; 2 free legendary sculptures daily |
| VIP 13 | First Troop Health bonus |
| VIP 14 | 3 free legendary sculptures daily — commonly cited as the clearest dividing line between accounts that can keep commanders maxed and accounts that fall behind |
| VIP 15 | ~1.5x resource production; first March Speed and Healing Speed bonuses; +100 Action Point limit |
| VIP 16–18 | Top bracket: up to ~1.65x resource production, +5% all damage (VIP 17–18), −15% healing resource cost, +400 Action Point limit |

> **PRIORITY:** Treat VIP progression as your single highest-priority permanent gem sink. Spend in this order: VIP 6 → VIP 10 → Books of Covenant (City Hall upgrades) → VIP 12 → Wheel of Fortune (once you have a real gem stockpile) → VIP 14.

## Section 7 — Governor Profile & Kingdom Titles

*Terminology note: Rise of Kingdoms does not have a separate "Governor skill tree." The Governor Profile is an information hub (nickname, ID, civilization, alliance, power, kills/honor, achievements, rankings). The actual skill-tree-with-talent-points system belongs to **Commanders** (Section 9), not to the Governor account itself.*

### Kingdom Titles

Titles are the real "governor politics" system. They're granted by top kingdom leadership (King, Queen, General, or Prime Minister, or a delegated title-giver) and must be requested and granted *before* starting the relevant action — they give no retroactive benefit. There's no cooldown; you hand the title back once you're done so the next person in the queue can use it.

| Title | Effect | Use it before... |
|---|---|---|
| King | +5% Troop Attack, +5% Defense, +5% Health | (top leadership; assigns Queen/General/Prime Minister) |
| Queen | +15% Gathering Speed | a gathering session |
| General | +5% Troop Attack, +5% Defense | combat |
| Prime Minister | +15% Resource Production, +10% Building Speed | a building queue |
| Justice | +5% Troop Attack, +10% March Speed | rallies/garrisons, especially in KvK |
| Duke | +5% Troop Defense, +10% Training Speed | training a large troop batch |
| Architect | +10% Building Speed | starting a construction upgrade |
| Scientist | +5% Research Speed, +10% Gold Gathering Speed | starting an Academy research task |

(Punitive titles — Traitor, Beggar, Exile, Slave, Sluggard, Fool — apply stat penalties and are handed out by leadership to rule-breakers.)

> **TIP:** Request a title by placing a personal map bookmark labeled with the title name (e.g. "Duke Please") and sharing your coordinates in Kingdom or Alliance chat where the title-giver is active. Titles can be stacked with matching consumable "runes" for extra time reduction on the same action.

## Section 8 — Commander System Basics

Commanders are named historical figures who lead marches, boost stats through skills and talents, and are required for nearly all combat and gathering actions.

### Roles a commander can fill

- **PvE hunting** — killing neutral Barbarians for XP and rewards.
- **PvE/PvP rally leader** — attacking Barbarian Forts, Passes, Holy Sites, or enemy cities as part of a rally.
- **Garrison/defense** — a primary + secondary pair assigned to your Wall (or an alliance stronghold) to auto-defend against incoming attacks.
- **Gathering** — sent to resource tiles to boost gathering speed and load.
- **Rally join/reinforcement** — supporting someone else's rally; here march speed and support/debuff talents matter more than raw offense.

### Rarity tiers

Four tiers, weakest to strongest: **Advanced → Elite → Epic → Legendary**. Advanced/Elite are cheap and fast to obtain but weak — mainly useful for dedicated gathering roles. Epic is the mid-game/F2P workhorse tier. Legendary is the hardest to obtain (mostly through gold/sovereign Tavern keys) and the strongest.

### Leveling mechanics

- Commanders gain **XP** from fighting Barbarians, Holy Site Guardians, and other neutral units, or from Experience Tomes.
- **Star level** (separate from XP level) is raised using **Starlight Sculptures**; each star raises the max XP level cap by 10, up to level 60 at 6 stars. Star level also unlocks additional skill slots — reaching **3-star** is the key threshold, since it unlocks secondary-commander pairing on marches.
- Every commander has exactly one active skill (usually triggers at 1000 Rage) plus 3–4 passive skills, each levelled 1 through 5 using Commander Sculptures.
- Epic and Legendary commanders get a 5th "Expertise/Mastery" skill, which only unlocks once all four base skills are at level 5.
- Fully maxing all four base skills costs roughly: **Legendary 690 sculptures, Epic 440, Elite 340, Advanced 240**. You need 10 sculptures of a given commander just to unlock them initially.

> **MISTAKE TO AVOID:** Not every commander benefits from being levelled evenly. Some (Cao Cao, El Cid, Guan Yu, Lu Bu, Tomyris) are strongest with a 5/1/1/1 skill spread, since only their first skill really matters — dumping sculptures evenly across all four skills wastes resources on commanders like these. Others (Attila, Chandragupta Maurya, Genghis Khan, Wu Zetian, Zenobia) are genuinely worth maxing 5/5/5/5.

### Secondary commander pairing

Once a commander reaches 3-star, it can be paired with a second commander on the same march — one primary, one secondary. **Only the primary commander's talent points apply in battle** — the secondary contributes only its active/passive skills, not its talents. Good pairings complement the primary's damage sequence with the secondary's debuffs or buffs.

> **F2P:** Don't invest talent points into a commander you intend to use purely as a secondary — those points have zero effect on combat. Save talent investment for primary commanders only.

## Section 9 — Talent Trees

Each commander draws from **3 of 15 total talent tree specialties**, organized into three colour-coded branches:

- **Red tree** — which troop type the commander buffs: Infantry, Cavalry, Archer, or a generalist path (Leadership/Integration) that buffs all troop types.
- **Yellow tree** — the commander's excelling activity: Garrison, Conquering, Gathering, Peacekeeping, Support, or Versatility.
- **Blue tree** — combat execution focus, most often Attack, Defense, Mobility, Support, or Skill.

**Talent points:** one is awarded every time a commander gains an XP level, plus bonus points at star levels 5 and 6, for a commonly cited maximum of **74 points** at level 60.

Rough groupings by yellow-tree specialty (useful for picking a commander for a specific job):

- **Garrison** (defense): Charles Martel, Constantine I, Eulji Mundeok, Hermann, Kusunoki Masashige, Pelagius, Richard I, Yi Seong-Gye.
- **Conquering** (rally/city attack): Baibars, Frederick I, Hannibal Barca, Julius Caesar, Mehmed II, Osman I, Saladin, Scipio Africanus.
- **Peacekeeping** (PvE/barbarians): Belisarius, Boudica, Cao Cao, Lohar, Minamoto no Yoshitsune.
- **Gathering**: Centurion, Cleopatra VII, Constance, Gaius Marius.
- **Skill** (the largest group — skill damage/rage generation): Baibars, Boudica, Constance, El Cid, Frederick I, Gaius Marius, Hermann, Kusunoki Masashige, Mehmed II, Minamoto no Yoshitsune, Osman I, Pelagius, Sun Tzu, Yi Seong-Gye.

> **MISTAKE TO AVOID:** Reassigning talent points is possible via a Talent Reset item, but it's expensive and scales with points already spent. Don't invest talents into a commander before you're sure of its intended role.

## Section 10 — Top Commander Pairings by Playstyle

*Note: the commander meta shifts with new "Prime" variants and new legendaries — treat rankings below as a solid current baseline, not a permanent tier list.*

**Rally leader (archer):** Nebuchadnezzar II + Gilgamesh for city-attack rallies (AOE hits up to 5 targets); Zhuge Liang + Hermann (Prime) is widely rated the top archer investment in the current meta; Yi Seong-Gye remains strong for barbarian-fort chains and versatile rally/garrison use.

**Rally leader (cavalry):** Attila + Genghis Khan for city attacks; Attila + Chandragupta Maurya as a garrison-assault variant.

**Rally leader (infantry):** Guan Yu, valued for AOE damage plus healing that sustains long rally fights; Charles Martel + Richard I is a classic all-around structure-defense pairing.

**Garrison/defense:** Richard I + Constantine I is described as a near-unkillable structure-defense setup; Wu Zetian (skill damage + healing + damage reduction) is another strong garrison pick.

**Open-field PvP:** Liu Che + Scipio Africanus (Prime) for infantry; Zhuge Liang + Hermann (Prime) for archers.

**Gathering:** Cleopatra VII (+30% stone gathering, +20% other resources), Seondeok (best late-game gold gatherer), Constance (bonus resources on completed gathers), Joan of Arc (gathering speed + troop load).

**PvE — Barbarians/Forts:** Boudica, Aethelflaed, Cao Cao, Minamoto no Yoshitsune, and Belisarius are the top named Barbarian Fort commanders, combining nuke damage with Peacekeeping talents that reduce the action-point cost per attack.

**Fast march / rally-join (F2P-friendly):** Lancelot + Dragon Lancer (Cavalry) is the easiest pairing to build up and directly boosts march speed; Hermann + Tomoe Gozen (Archer) is a solid alternative.

> **TIP:** Rally squads are overwhelmingly cavalry, because most premier rally-leader commanders are cavalry specialists whose skills are built around fast, high-damage assaults on Fort/City garrisons — and cavalry is fast enough that it won't slow the group's march.

## Section 11 — Legendary Commander Acquisition (Sculptures & Tavern Keys)

Commander Sculptures come in the same four rarities as commanders (Legendary/Epic/Elite/Advanced) and two types: **Universal** (usable on any commander of that rarity) and **Specific** (tied to one named commander).

### Where sculptures come from

- **Tavern Gold Keys** — open Golden Chests containing Legendary/Epic/Elite commanders and sculptures; you get roughly one free Golden Chest every 2 days, improvable by upgrading the Tavern. Older legendaries rotate into the Gold Key pool once superseded by newer releases.
- **Sovereign/Mythic Keys** — drop only Legendary commanders, including the newest ones not yet in Gold Keys. Obtained via gem bundles or through event quests (barbarian hunting, gathering, troop training); a free one is also given out periodically.
- **VIP daily chest bonus** — VIP 10 grants 1 free legendary sculpture/gold key daily; VIP 12 grants 2/day; VIP 14 grants 3/day.
- **VIP Shop** — roughly 2,000 gems per legendary sculpture, capped around 20/week; guides describe this as an inefficient use of gems compared with events.
- **Events** — Mightiest Governor (Section 17), Wheel of Fortune, Card King (lets you pick a specific commander's sculpture), Ark of Osiris, and other recurring events regularly offer sculptures as rewards.

> **PRIORITY:** Commit sculptures to one commander at a time rather than spreading them thin across many — the goal is unlocking a commander's full skill set (and its Expertise skill) quickly, not owning a wide roster of half-built commanders.

## Section 12 — Troops and Tech Tree

### Troop types

Four troop types, each led by Commanders, in a rock-paper-scissors relationship: **Cavalry beats Archers, Archers beat Infantry, Infantry beats Cavalry.** Siege is weak against every combat troop type.

- **Infantry** — the tank: highest defense/health, slowest march speed. Best for garrisons and city/fort defense; many infantry commanders carry healing talents suited to long fights.
- **Cavalry** — the offense/mobility unit: fastest march speed, well-rounded stats, single-target attack. Used for open-field combat and, above all, large-scale rally attacks.
- **Archers** — the damage/AOE unit: highest offensive output but low defense/health. Versatile across PvP, PvE, and (with the right commander) garrison defense.
- **Siege** — the utility unit: highest resource load (best for gathering and looting a captured city) but lowest combat stats and slowest march speed. Its unique combat use is bonus damage against Watchtowers/walls — sieges are never used in open field battles because they die instantly to any other troop type.

> **Terminology note:** Rise of Kingdoms troops only go up to **Tier 5 (T5)** — there is no T10/T11 system in this game (that belongs to a different, similarly-structured title called Kingshot). Don't confuse the two when researching further.

**Troop tiers T1–T5**, unlocked through Academy research, each exponentially stronger than the last:

- T1–T2 are cheap, fast, and only relevant in the earliest game.
- T3 is the first meaningful upgrade; all civilizations share identical T3 units.
- T4 is the "backbone" tier most accounts reach around 10 million power; each civilization gets a specialized signature unit at this tier (see Section 3 table).
- T5 is the endgame tier, unlocked around City Hall 25, typically not reached until roughly 45–50 million power — for F2P/low-spend accounts this can take about a year of steady play.

> **MISTAKE TO AVOID:** Don't insta-upgrade every troop the instant a new tier unlocks. Early on, train fresh troops of the new tier rather than converting old ones — you'll usually have spare capacity. Once at a steady state, upgrade tier N-2 into tier N instead of training from scratch.

### Tech tree

Research happens at the Academy, split into an **Economic** tree and a **Military** tree.

**Economic tree highlights:** Masonry (+15% construction speed at max) and Engineering (+35% construction speed at max) together cut build time roughly in half; Writing (+10% Academy research speed) and Mathematics (+15% Academy research speed) both compound into all future research, making them priority picks. The first three columns of the Economic tree can be unlocked for free simply by visiting Villages on the map.

**Military tree** is organized into progressive tiers, each unlocking new troop tiers and survivability/utility techs (Combat Tactics, Defensive Formation, Herbal Medicine act as universal buffs affecting all march types and are high priority once available; Medical Corps improves healing/recovery; Combined Arms and Encampment support large-scale war coordination). The top tier unlocks Tier 5 troops and requires both City Hall and Academy at level 25, plus a large stock of Books of Covenant.

> **PRIORITY (research order):** New accounts — rush T3 troops, grab free Village tech immediately. Early-mid game — finish T3 military techs (Combat Tactics/Defensive Formation/Herbal Medicine) before jumping tiers. Mid game — rush T4 in one chosen troop specialization rather than spreading research across all three combat types; deprioritize Siege tech unless you're playing a dedicated siege/support role. Late game — max core techs before attempting T5, and lean on alliance research donations and the Scientist title (+10% research speed) to get there faster.

## Section 13 — Rallies, Marches, and Formations

A **rally** is a coordinated group attack — the only way to attack Barbarian Forts and most fortified structures/cities (regular Barbarians can be attacked solo). Rally capacity scales with your **Castle** level.

- The **rally leader** sets the join timer and supplies the commanders whose buffs apply to the whole group; a **joiner's** own commander skills stop applying once merged into the rally — only their +March Speed and +Troop Capacity traits matter for getting there. Rally march speed is capped by the slowest troop type sent, so sending Siege into a rally slows the whole group down — a classic beginner mistake.
- **Garrison/reinforcement** is separate from rallying: alliance members can send troops to help defend an ally's city or alliance stronghold, capped by that structure's reinforcement capacity. A portion of reinforcing troops can be lost if the defended target falls, so reinforcing carries real risk.

**Troop composition by squad type:**

- **Rally squads** are overwhelmingly cavalry (see Section 10).
- **Field/roaming squads** are typically built around a single troop type matching your civilization and available commanders, optimized for open-field PvP skirmishes.
- **Garrison squads** are infantry-heavy, since Infantry has the best defense/health and many infantry commanders carry garrison-specific bonuses; cavalry is explicitly weak in a garrison role.
- **Siege** is kept separate: a dedicated high-tier siege force for cracking Watchtowers/walls during city or fort attacks, plus a cheap low-tier siege stack purely for map gathering.

> **MISTAKE TO AVOID:** Mixed-troop marches (all three combat types together) are common early on but fall off in value once your commanders reach roughly level 40 — march speed gets capped by your slowest unit, equipment can't optimize for three types at once, and most late-game research is type-specific. Settle on one main troop specialization as you scale up.

## Section 14 — PvE Content

- **Barbarians (non-Fort)** — levels roughly 1–12+, cost Action Points to attack, and drop speedups, gems, resources, XP, and Arrows of Resistance. AP cost per consecutive attack drops as you chain kills without returning to the city (up to a max 10 AP reduction), further boosted by Peacekeeping-talent commanders. Chain-farming with an AOE commander (Yi Seong-Gye, Sun Tzu) lets you clear several barbarians for the AP cost of one.
- **Barbarian Forts** — require a rally; scale in level, with each level demanding more troops and a higher troop tier to clear. They're the fastest way to level a commander and the main source of Books of Covenant needed for City Hall/Castle upgrades — prioritize Forts over plain Barbarians while covenant books are your bottleneck.
- **Barbarian Keep** — a boss room at the heart of a Barbarian Camp guarded by troops and Arrow Towers; clearing them raises an "alertness" meter, and at 100% the Barbarian Boss spawns, dropping an equipment-material chest, a Tome of Knowledge, keys, and a speedup.
- **Lost Kingdom map (KvK content)** — a separate cross-kingdom map that opens during Kingdom vs. Kingdom seasons, divided into escalating Zones. Sanctuaries (Zone 5) and Circles (Zone 6, functioning like Shrines) grant passive kingdom-wide or alliance-wide bonuses when captured and held; Altar Guardians spawn twice daily as high-value PvE targets dropping Runes, Blueprints, and XP.
- **Ceroli Crisis** — a 4-player co-op PvE event, unlocked at City Hall 10, fighting named bosses across 5 escalating difficulty tiers (Easy → Normal → Hard → Nightmare → Hell). Rewards scale with damage dealt; recruit known, reliable teammates rather than relying on public matchmaking.

> **TIP:** Once your Castle is maxed and covenant books are no longer your bottleneck, switch your farming focus from Barbarian Forts back to plain Barbarians for gems and speedups.

## Section 15 — Alliance Mechanics

### Ranks

- **R5 (Leader)** — one per alliance; full control over rules, diplomacy, and appointments.
- **R4 (Officer)** — the most privileged non-leader rank: can kick/promote/demote, accept or reject join requests, schedule alliance events, build alliance structures, view member coordinates, and start alliance tech research.
- **R2–R3** — base permissions plus alliance-wide messaging.
- **R1 (Member)** — alliance chat, giving Alliance Help, and leaving the alliance.

### Territory

Territory is built from a **Fortress**, **Flags**, and captured **Holy Sites**. More territory means more member slots, more room to teleport within, +25% gathering speed on your own territory, and better daily rewards. The central Fortress needs 20+ members and 500,000+ alliance power to build; an alliance can build up to 3 fortresses total, with each additional one requiring more members and power. Flags expand territory outward from existing borders (capped at 500 per alliance); every 10 flags built unlocks one more member slot but raises the cost of the next batch.

### Tech donation and credits

Donating resources or gold generates Technology Points that officers spend on the alliance tech tree (Development, Territory, War, and Alliance Skill categories). Donations are capped at 20 uses, refreshing every 30 minutes. There are two separate currencies: **Individual Credits** (personal, earned via Alliance Help and gold donations) and **Alliance Credits** (pooled, spent on fortresses/flags/tech by leadership).

> **TIP:** In the Alliance Shop, prioritize Passport Pages (needed for migration, Section 16) and VIP Points first, then speedups, keys, resource packs, and shields.

### Choosing an alliance

Alliances are either Open (join freely) or Application-based (leader/officer approval, generally more active). Match an alliance to your language, activity level, and playstyle — it's completely normal to switch alliances once you've found the right fit.

## Section 16 — Kingdom vs. Kingdom (KvK)

KvK — also called "The Lost Kingdom" for its earliest seasons — is large-scale cross-kingdom warfare in which groups of kingdoms are placed on a shared neutral map and fight for territory, honor, and major rewards. All alliances within a kingdom cooperate against the other kingdoms in the match.

- **Timing:** the first KvK season typically opens 85–105 days after a kingdom's founding, gated behind a roughly week-long prep event called **Eve of the Crusade**. KvK 1 runs about 60 days, KvK 2 about 50 days, with further seasons following every couple of months of "peacetime."
- **Entry requirement:** City Hall 16–17+ for early KvK seasons; newer formats (Season of Conquest) require City Hall 25+.
- **Matchmaking:** based on the combined power of a kingdom's strongest players, so new kingdoms are never matched against veteran ones; sometimes several kingdoms are grouped into one "camp" against another camp.
- **Named seasons that actually exist:** The Lost Kingdom (KvK 1 & 2), Light and Darkness (KvK 3), Desert Conquest, and the newer multi-chapter **Season of Conquest** format (chapters include Heroic Anthem, Strife of the Eight, and March of the Ages), which adds a Coalition system (cross-kingdom mega-alliance), a Camp system, Crystal Mine/Crystal Research Center buildings, a Bastion system tied to specific epic commanders, and a Hall of Heroes that resurrects roughly half of troops killed in PvP during the season once it ends.
- **Ancient Ruins / Altar of Darkness:** recurring limited-time occupation windows (Ancient Ruins open for 1 hour roughly every day and a half; Altar of Darkness for 1 hour roughly every 3.5 days) — holding them grants steady alliance and individual honor while occupied.
- **Honor:** tracked at Individual, Alliance, and Kingdom level, earned from barbarian kills, resource-point gathers, and fort destruction. Top individual finishers get a special skin; top alliances get sculptures/speedups/credits; only the top handful of kingdoms get kingdom-tier rewards.
- **Migration:** moving your city to a different kingdom using a **Passport Page**. Requires City Hall 16+, empty march queues, leaving your current alliance first, and stored resources under your Storehouse's protected capacity. You can only migrate to a kingdom that's been open 120+ days or is older than your own.

> **TIP:** During KvK, watch your alliance's flag and gate status closely, and use shields when going offline near the front line — getting "zeroed" (attacked down to 0 troops/resources) is almost always a timing/awareness failure, not a power gap.

## Section 17 — Major Recurring Events

- **Mightiest Governor** — the single most important recurring event: 6 daily stages (Troop Training → Defeat Barbarians → Gather Resources → Increase Power → Eliminate Enemies → Final Sprint). Rewards legendary sculptures on a sliding scale from top finishers down to roughly the top 50. Rewards drop off a cliff outside the top 10, so it's usually better to save resources for a push you can realistically win rather than over-spending for a marginal placement.
- **Ark of Osiris** — a biweekly, 30-vs-30 alliance eSport-style battleground fought over Obelisks, Shrines, Outposts, Altars, and a central "Ark" delivery objective. Split into Golden (top-power alliances) and Silver (everyone else) battlefields; reaching 10,000 individual points is the milestone for maximum personal reward regardless of the team's overall result.
- **Sunset Canyon** — a weekly-reset individual PvP arena where all participants use equalized Tier 3 troops, so only commander level, skills, gear, and talents matter. Five free daily attempts, plus paid Challenge Tickets.
- **Ceroli Crisis** — see Section 14.
- **Anniversary Festival** — an annual multi-event bundle (login streaks, personalized year-in-review recaps, returning-player rewards).
- Smaller recurring gem/reward events include Strategic Reserve (monthly), Canyon Clash, and quick puzzle-style events like Sharp Eyes and Quick Hands — all worth light, regular participation even without a top placement.

> **F2P:** Don't chase 1st place in ranked events — that tier belongs to heavy spenders. Aim for the top 50–100 bracket instead, which asks for far less effort but still pays out real gems and sculptures.

## Section 18 — Common Beginner Mistakes

1. Converting resource tokens/packs into raw resources before you need them, exposing them to raiding.
2. Leveling too many commanders at once instead of focusing on 2 primary commanders.
3. Over-investing sculptures into a Secondary Commander past the point where all 4 skills unlock — secondary commanders only contribute skills in battle, never talents.
4. Letting building, research, or training queues sit idle.
5. Delaying joining an alliance to "find the perfect one" — any active alliance beats none.
6. Ignoring City Hall in favor of evenly leveling minor buildings.
7. Overspending resources/speedups in ranked events you have no realistic shot at placing well in.
8. Committing to the wrong talent tree build on a commander, forcing an expensive Talent Reset to fix it.
9. Spreading commander sculptures too thin across many commanders instead of committing to one at a time.
10. Sending the wrong troop type into a rally, fort attack, or flag assault — most commander skills require a specific troop type to actually trigger.
11. Getting caught off guard during KvK by not protecting resources, not watching alliance flag/gate status, or not shielding before going offline near the front line.

## Section 19 — F2P Strategy Guide

**Gem spending priority:** VIP 6 → VIP 10 → Books of Covenant (City Hall upgrades) → VIP 12 → Wheel of Fortune (once you have a real stockpile) → VIP 14. Commander sculptures are the highest-leverage spend once your VIP track is on schedule.

**Free gem sources for active players:**
- Daily Objectives — a guaranteed chest from completing all daily tasks.
- Barbarian chaining with an AOE + Peacekeeping-talent commander setup, paired with a healer commander to sustain longer sessions.
- Alliance Gifts — chests shared whenever any alliance member makes a purchase; a strong reason to join a large, active alliance.
- Gem-node gathering on the map (unlocked via the Academy's Jewelry/Cutting-and-Polishing techs).
- Ranking events at the realistic top 50–100 tier rather than 1st place.
- Redeem codes shared through official social channels, especially around anniversaries.

**Best F2P-friendly commanders:** Sun Tzu (Epic, infantry AOE — a long-standing F2P staple), Aethelflaed (Legendary, repeatedly cited as the best F2P legendary for general PvE/PvP and Sunset Canyon), Yi Seong-Gye (long-time all-rounder for open-field, rally, and garrison use), Boudica and Lohar (peacekeeping/farming commanders that speed up daily barbarian grinding).

**Daily checklist:**
- Complete all Daily Objectives.
- Claim the free daily VIP point chest.
- Collect free Tavern chests.
- Keep every queue (building, research, training, gathering) active — never idle.
- Chain barbarians for gem drops without letting Action Points hit their cap.
- Claim Alliance Gift chests.

**Weekly/periodic checklist:**
- Check KvK Chronicles rewards regularly — these can expire if neglected.
- Track the event calendar (Ark of Osiris, Mightiest Governor, Strategic Reserve, puzzle events) and participate at least at a light-effort level.
- Save speedups, shields, teleports, and resources between major events rather than burning them piecemeal.

> **ADVANCED:** Once VIP, commanders, and equipment are established, the highest-leverage remaining moves are: specializing fully into one troop type rather than staying generalist, running a farm account funneled through the Trading Post, and timing all major gem spends around the "More Than Gems" and legendary-sculpture events rather than spending reactively.
